## 1. Mission experience

**THE THERMAL DOMINION — CODE TDX-22**. A high-density training compute board with two heat-producing compute dies (U1 CPU and U2 accelerator), multi-phase VRM, 8 distributed sensor nodes (TS1–TS8), primary and secondary heatsinks (HS1/HS2), two removable heatpipes/vapor-path members (HP1/HP2), a PWM/tach fan assembly (FAN1), an independent thermal watchdog and a detachable trusted thermal safety feedback jumper (W257). Mission: assemble; route; calibrate independent thermometry; qualify safe thermal behavior under idle, nominal, burst and sustained workload; find/fix three nonidentical faults; then complete supervised W257 recovery.

The primary educational insight is that **sensor value ≠ physical heat transfer ≠ cooling capacity ≠ protection authority**. A visibly spinning fan, a cyan heatmap or a green LED must never certify safety. Not automotive, medical, aerospace, real power electronics, or actual safety compliance. All operations are simulated.

## 8. Fan, throttle and safety semantics

Model actual fan airflow independently of PWM request and tachometer pulses. A stuck tach-high can misreport healthy RPM even while cooling is poor. Add independent thermal readings near U1/U2 and VRM; compare realistic thermal response under load to expected fan cooling. Validate fan start/stop, stuck rotor, intermittent connector, duty-to-flow curve, sensor open/short and thermal runaway. CPU throttling reduces modeled power over time but cannot replace repair; a valid safety latch inhibits protected high-heat workloads while diagnostic power and logging remain. Require transient response testing, not instantaneous simulated temperature teleportation.

## 9. Three faults (real state mutation required)

**F1 — U1-to-HS1 TIM delamination.** Modify actual thermal conductance, not just surface material. Under load U1 junction rises relative to HS1/ambient. Diagnose independent thermometry and heat flux, isolate supply, remove heatsink, replace the approved thermal pad/paste, clamp and recertify measured junction-to-heatsink resistance and transient response. No arbitrary UI click clears F1.

**F2 — FAN1 tach stuck HIGH and rotor partial stall.** Fan PWM may appear correct and tach may lie; actual airflow drops. Diagnose by independent heatsink temperature rise and cross-check thermal sensors. Replace/reconnect fan or approved tach path in safe state, verify real modeled airflow, PWM feedback, fan response and fresh heat-run. F2 differs from general bad thermistor failure and must not be solved by fake tach ticks.

**F3 — VRM thermal-via discontinuity.** Break approved thermal path under the Q-bank. Power electrical continuity may remain good while VRM hotspot rises due to higher thermal resistance. Probe the correct region, inspect L1–L20 and real via span; repair only legal geometry after isolation, then measure VRM heat reduction under repeated load transients and check ground/power for shorts.

Each fault must have deterministic ID and scenario epoch, discovered target, appropriate tools, allowable repair actions and fresh calibrated evidence. Evidence from an earlier fault epoch never substitutes for a later acceptance test.
