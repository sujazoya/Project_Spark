## Energy equation

`Eflywheel = 0.5 * J * Omega²`

## Usable kinetic energy

`Eusable = 0.5 * J * (OmegaCurrent² - OmegaMinimum²)`

Clamp usable energy to zero when below the defined minimum operating speed.

## Mechanical equation

`J * dOmega/dt = Tmotor - Tgenerator - Tloss`

Use a consistent torque sign convention.

## Charging behavior

- Electrical power drives the motor-generator in motoring mode.
- Motor torque accelerates the flywheel.
- Actual RPM increases gradually.
- Kinetic energy increases.
- Electrical losses are accounted for.
- Overspeed protection remains active.

## Discharging behavior

- Motor-generator enters generating mode.
- Electrical output comes from reduced rotor kinetic energy.
- Actual RPM decreases.
- Stored energy decreases.
- Output power remains finite.
- Minimum speed is respected.

Do not assign flywheel energy independently of actual RPM.

Do not let energy become negative.

## Simplified inertia-like control

`PinertiaRequest = -Kinertia * df/dt`

## Droop control

`PdroopRequest = Kdroop * (fReference - fActual)`

## Combined support request

`Prequest = PinertiaRequest + PdroopRequest + PdampingRequest`

These are conceptual control terms.

Use a physically consistent implementation with the actual network and converter dynamics.

## Limits

The request must be constrained by:

- Battery SOC
- DC-link availability
- Inverter current
- Converter power
- Thermal limits
- Electrical network conditions

## Important

Virtual inertia is a control response, not an additional physical flywheel.

Increasing its gain must not increase battery capacity or create new energy.
