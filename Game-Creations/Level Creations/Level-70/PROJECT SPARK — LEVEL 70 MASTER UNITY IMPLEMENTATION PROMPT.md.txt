# 10. DIFFERENTIAL-PRESSURE PHYSICS

The primary pressure cascade is:

- Cleanroom: +15 Pa relative to corridor.
- Airlock: +8 Pa relative to corridor.
- Corridor: 0 Pa gauge.

The cleanroom should normally be at higher pressure than the airlock.

The airlock should normally be at higher pressure than the corridor.

## IDEAL-GAS MASS RELATION

For a well-mixed room approximation:

`P = m * Rair * T / V`

where:

- P = absolute pressure.
- m = air mass.
- Rair = specific gas constant.
- T = absolute temperature.
- V = room volume.

## DYNAMIC PRESSURE

For a fixed-volume, near-isothermal approximation:

`dP/dt ≈ (Rair * T / V) * (mdotIn - mdotOut)`

For changing temperature, include the corresponding temperature term or solve the pressure state consistently.

## PRACTICAL SOLVER

Because cleanroom pressure differences are small relative to atmospheric pressure, a quasi-steady node-pressure solver may be numerically more suitable.

Either approach is acceptable if documented and validated.

## REQUIRED BEHAVIOR

- Increasing supply airflow can increase room pressure.
- Increasing return/exhaust airflow can lower room pressure.
- Door opening changes leakage area.
- Fan saturation limits pressure control.
- Filter loading changes operating flow.
- Relief damper limits excess pressure.
- The controller cannot maintain impossible pressure setpoints.

`Qleak = C * sign(DeltaP) * sqrt(abs(DeltaP))`

where C includes opening area and appropriate physical coefficients.

# 16. AIRBORNE PARTICLE TRANSPORT MODEL

Implement an authoritative particle inventory for:

For a well-mixed region:

`d(Nparticles)/dt = ParticleIn + ParticleGenerated - ParticleOut - ParticleRemoved`
